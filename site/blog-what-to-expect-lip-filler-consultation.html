What to expect at a lip filler consultation

Written by Eleana, Advanced Nurse Practitioner & independent prescriber

If you're thinking about lip fillers for the first time, the consultation is where everything starts. It's a calm, private conversation — no pressure, no commitment. Here's exactly what happens and how to make the most of it.

What a consultation is actually for

A consultation isn't a sales appointment. It's a proper clinical assessment. The purpose is to work out whether treatment is right for you, understand what you're hoping to achieve, and make sure any decision is an informed one. It's also your chance to meet the person who would be treating you, ask everything on your mind, and get a feel for whether the clinic is somewhere you trust.

Just as importantly, it's a two-way conversation. You're assessing us as much as we're assessing you. Nobody should feel rushed into a treatment, and a good consultation should leave you better informed even if you decide not to go ahead.

Medical history and suitability

Early on, we'll go through your medical history. This includes any conditions you have, medicines and supplements you take, allergies, previous aesthetic treatments and whether you're pregnant or breastfeeding. It might feel like a lot of questions, but it matters: some situations mean treatment isn't suitable, or that it's better to wait.

This is the part that makes nurse-led care different. A qualified nurse prescriber is trained to spot when something in your history needs a closer look, and to make a safe, clinical judgement rather than simply saying yes. Being honest here is genuinely in your interest — it's how we keep you safe.

Talking through your goals

Next comes the nicer part: what you actually want. Some people want a little more definition or hydration, some want to balance out a subtle unevenness, and some simply aren't sure and want to talk it through. There are no wrong answers.

It helps to describe the result you're after in your own words, and a photo of a look you like can be useful for showing the sort of shape or fullness you have in mind. We'll then talk honestly about what's realistic for your lips. Every face is different, and the aim is always a natural, balanced result that suits you — not a copy of someone else.

Assessing your lip shape and symmetry

We'll look closely at your lips: their shape, proportions, the border, how they move when you smile and talk, and any natural asymmetry. Most people have some degree of unevenness between the two sides, which is completely normal.

This assessment shapes everything that follows. It tells us what's achievable, where careful placement could help, and how to keep the result in harmony with the rest of your face. We'll explain what we're seeing so you understand the thinking, rather than just being told what to do.

The honest conversation — including "no" or "not yet"

A responsible clinic will sometimes say no. That might mean declining treatment altogether, suggesting a different approach, or recommending you wait — for example if your expectations aren't achievable safely, if your history needs review, or if a previous treatment needs time to settle first.

Hearing "not yet" can feel disappointing, but it's a good sign. It means the person in front of you is putting your safety and your result ahead of a booking. You should never feel talked into anything, and you should always feel able to ask "what would you do?" and get a straight answer.

Product, amount and why we discuss it

If treatment is suitable, we'll talk about the type of product that would be used, roughly how much, and why. We keep things conservative — it's easier to build gradually over time than to overdo it, and a subtle result almost always looks better and more natural.

We'll explain the approach in plain language, including how it works, how long results tend to last and what the treatment itself involves. You'll have time to ask questions before any decision is made. You can read more about our approach on the lip fillers page.

A preview of aftercare

We'll also give you a sense of what recovery looks like, so there are no surprises. Some swelling and tenderness in the first few days is normal, and lips can take a couple of weeks to fully settle. Knowing this in advance helps you plan around any events and judge your result at the right time. Our full lip filler aftercare guide walks through the first few days in more detail.

It's free, and there's no pressure

Our consultations are free and carry no obligation. You won't be pushed to book on the day, and taking time to think it over is always welcome — in fact, we encourage it. Many people go away, consider everything and come back when they feel ready. Some decide it's not for them, and that's a perfectly good outcome too.

What to bring and what to ask

To get the most from your appointment, it helps to come prepared:

  • A list of any medicines and supplements you take, and relevant medical history
  • A photo of a look you like, if you have one
  • Any questions or worries, written down so you don't forget them on the day

Good questions to ask include: are you a qualified prescriber; is treatment suitable for me; what result is realistic; what does aftercare involve; and what happens if I'm not happy or something doesn't feel right. A clinic that welcomes these questions is a clinic worth trusting.

Feeling nervous? That's normal

If it's your first time, feeling a bit anxious is completely understandable. There's nothing to prove and no decision to rush. The consultation exists precisely so you can slow down, ask everything and leave feeling informed — whether or not you choose to go ahead. When you're ready, you can get in touch to arrange a chat.

Common questions

How long does a lip filler consultation take?

Usually around twenty to thirty minutes. It covers your medical history, your goals, an assessment of your lips and an honest conversation about whether treatment is right for you.

Is the consultation free and do I have to book treatment?

The consultation is free and there is no obligation to go ahead. It is a chance to ask questions and decide in your own time. You are never pressured to book on the day.

Can I have treatment on the same day as my consultation?

Sometimes, if you are suitable, well informed and happy to proceed. Many people prefer to take time to think it over first, and that is always fine.

What should I bring to my consultation?

Bring a list of any medicines you take, details of relevant medical history, and any questions you have. A photo of a look you like can help explain your goals.
