Dermal fillers vs anti-wrinkle injections: which do you need?

These are two of the most common non-surgical treatments, and they are often confused. They do very different jobs. This guide explains what each one actually does, what it treats, how long it lasts, and how a consultation decides what's right for you.

The core difference

The simplest way to understand it: dermal fillers add, and anti-wrinkle injections relax.

Dermal fillers are a gel, usually made from hyaluronic acid — a substance your skin already produces — that is placed under the skin to add volume, restore structure or improve hydration. They physically fill or support an area, so the change is there as soon as the product is in place.

Anti-wrinkle injections work in the opposite way. They use a botulinum toxin that temporarily relaxes the small muscles responsible for certain expressions. When the muscle moves less, the lines it creates soften. Nothing is being added for volume — the effect comes from calming movement.

You may hear people casually call anti-wrinkle injections "Botox". That is a brand of prescription-only botulinum toxin, and by law prescription medicines can't be advertised to the public, which is why clinics describe the treatment generically. It is the same category of treatment; the point here is simply that it is not the same thing as filler.

What each one treats

Where dermal fillers are used

Because fillers add volume and structure, they suit concerns where an area has lost fullness or where you want more definition. Common areas include:

  • Lips — for subtle volume, shape or hydration
  • Cheeks — to restore volume that softens with age
  • Chin and jaw — for balance and definition
  • Nasolabial lines — the folds running from nose to mouth
  • Tear troughs — the hollow under the eyes, an advanced area assessed carefully

Where anti-wrinkle injections are used

Because they relax muscle movement, anti-wrinkle injections suit dynamic lines — the ones that appear when you make an expression. Common uses include:

  • Frown lines between the brows
  • Forehead lines
  • Crow's feet at the corners of the eyes

The same type of treatment can also be used for jaw slimming (relaxing the muscle used for clenching) and for excessive sweating. These are assessed individually, as suitability varies from person to person.

A quick way to tell them apart

If your concern is about volume or structure — thinner lips, flatter cheeks, a softer jawline, deeper folds — that usually points towards filler. If your concern is about lines that appear when you move — frowning, raising your brows, smiling — that usually points towards anti-wrinkle injections. Static lines that are visible even when your face is still can be more complex, and sometimes a combination is discussed.

How long each one lasts

Longevity varies with the product used, the area treated, and your own body, so no one can promise an exact figure. As a general guide, hyaluronic acid dermal fillers tend to last several months to well over a year depending on the area and product. Anti-wrinkle injections are temporary and the muscle-relaxing effect wears off over a period of months, after which movement — and the lines — gradually return. Both are maintained with repeat treatment rather than being permanent, which many people see as a benefit: your face is never locked into one decision.

Can you have both?

Yes — and it's common. Because the two treatments address different things, they're often used together as part of a considered plan rather than as an either/or choice. For example, someone might soften frown lines with anti-wrinkle injections while restoring cheek volume with filler. Whether to combine them, in what order, and whether to treat on the same day or across separate visits is a clinical decision, not a menu choice. It's made at your assessment based on your face and your goals.

Why a consultation decides what's right

It's easy to arrive having already decided which treatment you want, often from something you've seen online. The honest answer is that the right treatment can only be decided after a proper assessment. Sometimes the treatment people expect isn't the one that suits their concern, and being told that clearly is part of good, safe care.

Choosing a qualified medical prescriber also matters for safety. A prescriber can assess suitability properly, manage any complications, and give you a genuine point of contact afterwards. Common questions

Is "Botox" the same as dermal filler?

No. People often use that word casually to mean anti-wrinkle injections, but it's a prescription-only botulinum toxin brand that relaxes muscles to soften lines. Dermal fillers are a separate product, usually hyaluronic acid, that adds volume and structure. They work in completely different ways.

Can I have dermal fillers and anti-wrinkle injections at the same time?

In many cases yes, and they're often combined because they address different concerns. Suitability, sequencing and whether to treat on the same day or across separate appointments is decided at a consultation and assessment with a nurse prescriber.

Which one is better for forehead lines?

Lines that appear when you move, such as frown and forehead lines, are usually addressed with anti-wrinkle injections, which relax the muscle. What's right for you can only be confirmed at a personal assessment.

How do I know which treatment I need?

The clearest way is a consultation. Eleana will assess your face at rest and in movement, talk through what's realistic, and recommend the approach that fits your concern rather than starting from a treatment you've already picked.
