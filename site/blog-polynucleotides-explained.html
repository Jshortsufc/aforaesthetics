Polynucleotides explained

Polynucleotides are one of the most talked-about treatments in aesthetics right now. Here's a calm, honest look at what they actually are, how they differ from fillers and skin boosters, and who they tend to suit.

What are polynucleotides?

Polynucleotides are a type of regenerative injectable treatment. The active ingredient is made up of purified DNA fragments, which are carefully processed so they can be used safely in the skin. Rather than adding volume or reshaping a feature, they work with your own skin to support its repair processes, helping with hydration, firmness and overall skin quality over time.

Because they focus on the condition of the skin itself, polynucleotides sit in the same broad family as other skin boosters — treatments designed to improve how skin looks and feels rather than to change its shape. They are an increasingly popular option, and the science around regenerative aesthetics continues to develop.

How they differ from fillers and Profhilo

It's easy to lump all injectables together, but they do quite different jobs.

Versus dermal fillers

Dermal fillers are used to add volume, structure or definition to a specific area — for example, restoring lost volume or supporting a feature. Polynucleotides don't do this. They aim to improve the quality of the skin rather than to fill or reshape it.

Versus Profhilo and other skin boosters

Profhilo and similar hyaluronic-acid-based skin boosters mainly deliver deep hydration and a boost to the skin's own support structures. Polynucleotides are a little different again: their focus is on supporting the skin's natural repair and renewal. Some people use them alongside or instead of other boosters, depending on their skin and their goals. There's no single "best" option — it comes down to an individual assessment.

What are polynucleotides used for?

Polynucleotides are used in several areas, most commonly:

  • The under-eye and tear-trough area. Polynucleotides under the eyes are one of the most popular uses, because the skin here is thin, delicate and often shows tiredness first.
  • Overall skin quality on the face. Many people choose a polynucleotide skin treatment to support hydration, texture and firmness across the face.
  • Other areas of concern where skin quality and condition are the priority, as assessed at consultation.

You can see how they fit alongside our wider range of advanced treatments, which are always matched to the individual rather than sold as a one-size-fits-all fix.

What does a typical course look like?

Polynucleotides are usually given as a short course of sessions spaced a few weeks apart, rather than as a single one-off appointment. This staged approach gives the skin time to respond between visits. After the initial course, results are reviewed and any ongoing maintenance is discussed based on how your skin has responded.

What to expect and downtime

Treatment involves a series of small injections into the target area. Most people find it very manageable. Afterwards you may notice some redness, small raised areas or minor bruising at the injection points, which generally settle over a day or two. Downtime is usually minimal, and you'll be given clear aftercare advice to follow.

As with any regenerative treatment, results build gradually over the course rather than appearing overnight, so a little patience helps.

Who do polynucleotides suit?

They tend to appeal to people who want to support the quality and condition of their skin — including tired-looking under-eyes — rather than change the shape of a feature. Whether they're suitable for you depends on your skin, your medical history and your goals, which is exactly what a consultation is for.

Common questions

Are polynucleotides the same as dermal filler?

No. Dermal fillers add volume and structure to an area, while polynucleotides are a regenerative skin treatment that supports the quality, hydration and condition of the skin itself rather than reshaping it.

Can polynucleotides be used under the eyes?

The under-eye and tear-trough area is one of the most popular uses, as the skin here is thin and delicate. Suitability is always assessed individually at a consultation before any treatment is offered.

How many sessions will I need?

Polynucleotides are usually given as a short course of sessions spaced a few weeks apart, then reviewed. The exact plan depends on your skin and goals and is discussed at your consultation.

Is there much downtime?

Downtime is usually minimal. Some redness, small raised areas or minor bruising at the injection points can occur and generally settle over a day or two. You will be given clear aftercare advice.
