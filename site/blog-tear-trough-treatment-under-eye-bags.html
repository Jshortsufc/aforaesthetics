Tear trough treatment for under-eye bags and dark circles

Tired-looking eyes are one of the most common things people ask about. This guide explains what the tear trough is, why hollows and shadows appear, and when non-surgical treatment can help — honestly, including when it can't.

What is the tear trough?

The tear trough is the natural groove that runs from the inner corner of the eye downwards and outwards along the top of the cheek. In a well-rested, youthful face it is barely noticeable. When it becomes deeper or more shadowed, it can make you look tired even when you feel fine, and it is often the first thing people point to when they say they want to "look less exhausted".

Why hollows, shadows and bags appear

Several things can contribute, often at the same time:

  • Ageing and volume loss. Over time the fat pads that cushion the under-eye area shift and shrink, and the skin thins. This can create a hollow, and light falling into that hollow reads as a dark shadow.
  • Genetics. Some people simply inherit a deeper tear trough or thinner under-eye skin, and may notice shadows from a young age regardless of how much they sleep.
  • Skin quality and pigmentation. Thin skin can allow the bluish tone of the blood vessels beneath to show through, and some people have genuine pigmentation in this area.
  • Under-eye bags. Puffiness can be caused by fat pads pushing forward, fluid retention, or lifestyle factors. This is different from a hollow, and it does not always respond to the same approach.

Understanding which of these is driving your concern is the whole point of a proper assessment, because the right treatment — or the decision not to treat — depends on the cause.

Non-surgical options

Hyaluronic acid tear trough filler

The most common non-surgical treatment for a hollow tear trough is a hyaluronic acid dermal filler. Hyaluronic acid is a substance found naturally in the skin. Placed carefully and in very small amounts deep in the tear trough, it restores lost volume and lifts the hollow, so light no longer pools there. The result, when suitable, is a softer shadow and a fresher, less tired appearance rather than a dramatic change.

It is worth being clear about what this does and does not do. Filler adds structure to a hollow; it does not lighten true pigmentation and it will not remove genuine fat-pad bags. Because the under-eye holds onto fluid easily, a light, conservative approach matters far more here than volume.

Polynucleotides for skin quality

Where the concern is more about crêpey, thin or dull under-eye skin than a hollow, polynucleotides can be a better fit. These work on skin quality rather than adding volume, helping to support and condition the delicate skin around the eye. In some cases they are used alongside or instead of filler. You can read more in our guide to polynucleotides explained. Which option suits you is decided at assessment, not in advance.

Who is — and isn't — a good candidate

This is a delicate, advanced area, and honesty here matters more than in almost any other treatment. Some people suit tear trough filler and some genuinely do not.

People who tend to respond well have mild to moderate hollowing, good skin elasticity and no significant puffiness. People who may not be suitable include those with pronounced fat-pad bags, very lax or loose skin, a strong tendency to fluid retention, or shadows that are mostly pigmentation rather than a hollow. In some of these cases filler can make puffiness look worse, so a responsible practitioner will tell you it is not the right option for you rather than treat anyway.

There is no way to know which group you fall into without a face-to-face assessment. If a clinic offers to treat your under-eyes without examining them properly, that is a warning sign. You can arrange an honest, no-obligation assessment through our free consultation.

What to expect and downtime

Treatment is carried out with a fine needle or a cannula, and a numbing option is usually available for comfort. Afterwards, swelling and bruising are both possible in this area — the under-eye is delicate and can react more than you might expect. Some people have very little downtime; others notice puffiness or a bruise for a few days to a couple of weeks. Because the area can hold fluid, it is best to judge your final result once everything has fully settled rather than in the first few days.

Results are not permanent, and how long they last varies from person to person. There are no guarantees on outcome, and a good consultation will set realistic expectations rather than promise a specific result.

Why this area needs an experienced medical injector

The tear trough sits close to important blood vessels, and the skin here is thin and unforgiving of poor technique. That combination is exactly why it is considered an advanced treatment. An experienced medical injector understands the anatomy, selects the right patients, uses the right product in the right plane, and — just as importantly — knows how to recognise and manage a complication should one arise.

Common questions

Does tear trough filler get rid of dark circles completely?

Not always. Where dark circles are caused by a hollow casting a shadow, adding volume can soften that shadow. Where the darkness comes from pigmentation or thin skin showing the vessels beneath, filler makes less difference. An honest assessment will tell you which type you have and whether filler is likely to help.

Is tear trough filler safe?

The under-eye is a delicate, advanced area with important blood vessels nearby, so it carries more risk than many other treatments. In experienced medical hands, with careful patient selection and technique, it is performed routinely, but no treatment is without risk. This is why it should only be carried out by an experienced medical injector who can assess your suitability and manage any complication.

How long does the swelling last?

Some swelling or minor bruising is possible and usually settles over several days to a couple of weeks. The under-eye area can hold onto fluid, so it is best to judge the final result once everything has fully settled rather than in the first few days.

Am I a good candidate for tear trough filler?

Some people suit it and some do not. Mild to moderate hollowing with good skin quality tends to respond well. Significant puffiness, very lax skin or a tendency to fluid retention may mean filler is not the right option. The only way to know is a face-to-face assessment, and a responsible clinic will tell you if it is not suitable for you.
