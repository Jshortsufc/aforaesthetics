Anti-wrinkle injections explained

Written by Eleana, Advanced Nurse Practitioner & independent prescriber

A plain-English guide to anti-wrinkle (botulinum toxin) injections — what they are, how they work, and how to choose a safe, qualified practitioner.

This page is general information, not an advertisement. Anti-wrinkle injections use a prescription-only medicine, which by law cannot be promoted to the public — so we don't advertise, brand or price it here. You're welcome to book a free consultation to discuss whether it's suitable for you.

What they are

Anti-wrinkle injections use a prescription-only medicine to relax the muscles that create certain expression lines, softening their appearance over time. It's a common non-surgical treatment when carried out carefully, by a qualified prescriber, and with realistic expectations.

Areas often discussed

  • Frown lines between the brows
  • Forehead lines
  • Crow's feet around the eyes

Whether the treatment is suitable for you — and which areas — is only ever confirmed at a face-to-face consultation and assessment.

Why a prescriber matters

Because it's a prescription-only medicine, it must by law be prescribed by a suitably qualified prescriber after a face-to-face assessment. At A for Aesthetics that's Eleana, an Advanced Nurse Practitioner and independent prescriber with an MSc in advanced clinical practice and eight years' experience — so the person assessing you, prescribing and carrying out any treatment is one and the same clinician, not a beauty therapist working to someone else's prescription.

How to choose a safe practitioner

  • Check they're a registered medical professional (for example NMC-registered) and an independent prescriber.
  • Ask whether they assess and prescribe in person, and can manage complications if they arise.
  • Look for accreditation such as Save Face, and proper insurance.
  • Be wary of anyone advertising a prescription-only medicine by brand, or offering it without a consultation — both are against the rules.

What to expect afterwards

  • Results develop gradually, typically over one to two weeks.
  • Aftercare usually includes staying upright and avoiding rubbing the area for the rest of the day.
  • A review can be arranged to check how it has settled.

Common questions

What areas can it treat?

Common areas include frown lines between the brows, forehead lines and crow's feet around the eyes. Suitability is assessed individually at a consultation.

When will I see results?

Results tend to develop gradually over one to two weeks.

Is a consultation required first?

Yes. It's a prescription-only medicine, so a face-to-face assessment is required to confirm suitability before anything is arranged.
