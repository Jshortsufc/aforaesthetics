Bruising after fillers: how to reduce it and cover it

Written by Eleana, Advanced Nurse Practitioner & independent prescriber

A little bruising after fillers is one of the most common things people ask about, and one of the least worrying. Here's an honest look at why it happens, how to lower the chance of it, how long it takes to heal, and how to cover it neatly once the skin has closed.

Why bruising happens

Just under the surface of your skin sits a network of very small blood vessels. Fillers are placed with a fine needle, and if that needle passes near one of these tiny vessels, a small amount of blood can leak into the surrounding tissue. That's a bruise. It's a normal, harmless part of any injectable treatment and it can't be ruled out completely, even in careful, experienced hands. Areas with a richer blood supply, such as the lips, tend to bruise a little more readily than others.

So while a skilled injector can reduce the likelihood, no one can promise you won't bruise. If you do, it isn't a sign that anything has gone wrong.

How to lower the risk

You can't guarantee bruise-free treatment, but there are sensible steps that tip the odds in your favour. Where any of these involve medication or supplements, check with the relevant health professional first.

  • Ease off alcohol. Alcohol thins the blood and widens blood vessels, so it's worth avoiding it for a day or two before your appointment, and for a day or so afterwards.
  • Pause non-essential blood-thinning supplements where appropriate. Things like fish oil, high-dose vitamin E and some other supplements can make bruising more likely. Where it's medically appropriate, pausing them in the days beforehand can help. The same applies to non-essential aspirin taken casually rather than on medical advice.
  • Never stop prescribed medicines. If you take a prescribed blood-thinner, or aspirin that a doctor has told you to take, do not stop it. Instead, tell your practitioner at your consultation so it can be taken into account. Your prescribed treatment always comes first.
  • Consider arnica. Some people use arnica, taken or applied around the time of treatment, in the hope of reducing bruising. Evidence is mixed, but many find it helpful and it's widely used.
  • Time it around events. If you have a wedding, holiday or big occasion coming up, book at least a couple of weeks beforehand. That gives any bruising or swelling plenty of time to fade, so you're not relying on luck.

At your consultation, Eleana, an Advanced Nurse Practitioner and independent prescriber, will go through your medical history and any medicines or supplements you take, so your treatment is planned around them safely.

How long bruising takes to heal

Most bruises fade within a few days to around two weeks. As a bruise heals it usually changes colour, from purple or blue to green and then yellow, before disappearing. That colour change is completely normal and simply shows the body clearing it away. Bruising on the lips can sometimes take a touch longer than other areas because the tissue there is delicate and well supplied with blood.

How to cover a bruise with makeup

Once the skin has fully closed and there are no open pinpoint marks, usually from the day after treatment, you can cover a bruise with makeup. Wait until you're sure the surface has healed over, and always work with clean hands and clean brushes to keep the area hygienic.

  • Colour-correct first. A colour-correcting concealer helps neutralise the tone. A fresh, bluish bruise is often calmed by a peach or yellow-toned corrector; a yellow-green healing bruise is helped by a slightly lavender tone.
  • Layer lightly. Follow with a concealer close to your skin tone, then your usual foundation. Thin layers look far more natural than one thick one.
  • Be gentle. Press or dab makeup on rather than rubbing, so you don't disturb the healing skin or the settling filler.

When to seek advice

Ordinary bruising doesn't need any action beyond a little patience. Do get in touch with your practitioner, though, if you have swelling or bruising that keeps getting worse after the first couple of days rather than settling, severe or increasing pain, or any blanching, white patches, or skin that looks dusky or mottled in or around the treated area. These are uncommon, but they should be assessed promptly by someone qualified to manage them. If you're ever unsure, it's always better to get in touch and ask.

Common questions

Why do I bruise after fillers?

Bruising happens when a needle passes near one of the many tiny blood vessels under the skin and a small amount of blood leaks into the surrounding tissue. It's common, harmless and impossible to rule out completely, even with a careful, experienced injector.

How can I reduce the chance of bruising?

Avoiding alcohol for a day or two beforehand, and where medically appropriate pausing non-essential blood-thinning supplements such as fish oil and high-dose vitamin E, can lower the risk. Never stop prescribed blood-thinning medicine. Some people also use arnica. Booking a couple of weeks before an event gives any bruising time to fade.

How long does bruising after fillers last?

Most bruising fades within a few days to about two weeks. It often changes colour as it heals, from purple to green or yellow, which is a normal part of the process. Lip bruising can take a little longer than some other areas.

Can I cover a filler bruise with makeup?

Yes, once the skin has fully closed and there are no open marks, usually the day after treatment, you can cover a bruise with concealer. A colour-correcting concealer under your usual foundation works well. Apply gently and don't rub the area.
