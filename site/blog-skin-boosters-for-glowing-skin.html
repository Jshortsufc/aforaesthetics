Skin boosters for glowing skin: how they work

Written by Eleana, Advanced Nurse Practitioner & independent prescriber

If your skin looks a little dull, dry or crepey but you don't want to change your shape, a skin booster may be what you're after. Here's a calm, practical look at what skin boosters in Sheffield actually do, and how they differ from the injectables people often confuse them with.

What skin boosters actually are

Skin boosters are injectable treatments that work on the quality of your skin rather than its shape. Most are based on hyaluronic acid or polynucleotides, and they are placed in small amounts so the product spreads through an area to improve hydration, firmness, texture and glow. Profhilo is one well-known example, and polynucleotide treatments work in a related way to support the skin from within. Rather than filling a single spot, they condition the whole area, which is why people describe the result as healthier-looking skin rather than a change in their features.

How they differ from filler and anti-wrinkle injections

This is the part most people find confusing, so it's worth being clear. A dermal filler is placed in a specific location to add volume or structure, for example to a cheek or a chin. A skin booster does the opposite job, it disperses across a wider area to improve the skin itself and is not used to add volume. Anti-wrinkle injections are different again, they relax the muscles that create movement lines. A skin booster doesn't freeze movement and it doesn't reshape, it simply improves skin condition. Many people have more than one of these at different times for different reasons, which is something to talk through at a consultation.

A short course, with results that build

Skin boosters are usually given as a short course of sessions a few weeks apart, and the results build gradually over the following weeks as hydration and skin quality improve. That slow build is a genuine strength, but it does mean timing matters. If you have a wedding, holiday or other event in mind, start well before it rather than in the final week, so the results have time to settle. Your plan, including how many sessions and how far apart, is tailored to your skin.

Who they suit

Skin boosters tend to suit people who feel their skin looks tired, dehydrated or has lost a little bounce, and who want a refreshed look without changing their shape. They can be used on the face, and depending on the product, areas such as the neck. Whether any treatment is right for you depends on your skin, your health and your goals, and that is assessed individually. Eleana will be honest if a different approach would serve you better.

Aftercare in brief

Aftercare is straightforward. You may see small raised bumps or a little redness at the injection points for a short while, which settles. It's sensible to keep the area clean, avoid heavy exercise, saunas and makeup for the first day, and stay out of strong sun while things settle. You'll be given full written aftercare on the day.

Common questions

What are skin boosters?

Skin boosters are injectable treatments, usually based on hyaluronic acid or polynucleotides, that spread through an area to improve hydration, firmness, texture and glow. They condition the skin rather than adding shape or volume.

Are skin boosters the same as dermal filler?

No. Dermal filler is placed to add volume or structure in a specific spot. A skin booster disperses across a wider area to improve the quality of the skin itself, so it is not used to change your shape.

How soon will I see results?

Skin boosters work gradually. Most people notice changes over several weeks as hydration and skin quality build, which is why it helps to start a course well before any event rather than days beforehand.

How long do skin boosters last?

It varies from person to person and by the product used. Results typically last some months before a maintenance session is suggested. Eleana will give you a realistic timeline at your consultation.

Is there much downtime?

Most people return to normal activities the same day. You may have small raised bumps or a little redness at the injection points for a short while. Your aftercare advice covers what to expect and what to avoid.
