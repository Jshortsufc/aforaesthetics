Do lip fillers hurt? What to expect

Written by Eleana, Advanced Nurse Practitioner & independent prescriber

It is one of the most common questions before a first appointment, and a fair one. The honest answer is that lip filler is usually very manageable rather than genuinely painful. Here is what the sensation is actually like, how numbing works, and how a calm, nurse-led approach keeps you comfortable throughout.

So, does it actually hurt?

For most people, no, not in the way they expect. The lips are a sensitive area, so it is natural to feel a little apprehensive, but between the numbing used and a careful technique, the vast majority of clients describe the experience as very tolerable. You are likely to feel some pressure and, at a few points, a small scratch or sting, but not sustained pain. Everyone is different, and your comfort is checked throughout.

How numbing keeps you comfortable

Comfort is managed in two ways, and they work together:

  • Topical numbing cream. Before anything begins, a numbing cream is applied to the lips and left to take effect. This takes the edge off the initial sensation so the lips already feel dulled by the time treatment starts.
  • Anaesthetic within the filler. Many of the fillers used contain a local anaesthetic, which adds further comfort as the treatment progresses. In practice, the lips tend to feel increasingly numb as the appointment goes on.

Together these keep the sensation gentle for almost everyone. If you are particularly anxious about discomfort, say so at your consultation, it is something we plan for rather than push through.

What the sensation is like, step by step

Knowing what to expect often removes most of the worry. A typical appointment feels like this:

  • Before. Numbing cream goes on and you relax for a short while as it works.
  • During. You may feel a small scratch as each injection point is treated, along with some pressure or a squeezing sensation as the filler is placed. It is brief at each point rather than continuous.
  • After the numbing. As sensation returns over the next hour or two, the lips feel tender and a little swollen, similar to having bumped your lip.

The treatment itself is usually quick, and the careful, unhurried pace of a nurse-led appointment makes a real difference to how it feels.

Comfort afterwards

Once the anaesthetic wears off, mild tenderness and swelling are normal and expected. Some people notice a little bruising too. This is usually a dull, tender feeling rather than real pain, and it settles over a day or two. Gentle aftercare helps: stay hydrated, avoid heavy exercise, alcohol and heat for the first 24 hours, and avoid pressing or massaging the lips unless advised. For the full routine, see our guide to lip filler aftercare.

If anything feels beyond ordinary tenderness, you always have a point of contact. Because your treatment is carried out by Eleana, an Advanced Nurse Practitioner and independent prescriber, the person who treated you is also the person able to advise and, if ever needed, manage any concern safely.

How a nurse-led approach helps

Discomfort is not only about numbing. An experienced practitioner who works precisely, checks in with you and does not rush tends to make the whole experience calmer and more comfortable. At A for Aesthetics every treatment starts with a free consultation, so by the time any injection happens you know exactly what is planned and feel at ease. Common questions

Do lip fillers hurt?

Most people find lip filler very manageable rather than genuinely painful. A numbing cream is used beforehand and many fillers contain a local anaesthetic, so the lips are largely numb during treatment. You may feel a small scratch or pressure at points, but any discomfort is usually brief and mild.

What numbing is used for lip fillers?

At A for Aesthetics a topical numbing cream is applied before treatment, and the fillers used contain a local anaesthetic that adds further comfort as the treatment progresses. Together these keep the sensation gentle for the vast majority of people.

Does it hurt afterwards?

Once the numbing wears off, mild tenderness, swelling and sometimes a little bruising are normal for a day or two and then settle. It is usually a dull, tender feeling rather than real pain, and it eases quickly with gentle aftercare.

How can I make lip filler more comfortable?

Choosing an experienced, unhurried practitioner makes a real difference. Eat beforehand, arrive relaxed, and tell your practitioner if you feel anxious so they can pace the treatment and check in with you. A calm, careful nurse-led approach keeps the experience comfortable.
