Lip filler swelling: what's normal, day by day

Written by Eleana, Advanced Nurse Practitioner & independent prescriber

Some swelling after lip filler is completely normal, and it can be a bit alarming if you weren't expecting it. Here's a calm, realistic look at how the days after your treatment usually go, what's normal, what isn't, and how to keep the swelling comfortable while your lips settle.

Why lips swell after filler

Swelling is your body's natural response to being gently injected, and to the filler drawing in a little water as it settles. It's expected, not a sign that something has gone wrong. How much you swell varies from person to person and can differ from one appointment to the next, so try not to compare your first day too closely with someone else's.

A realistic day-by-day timeline

Straight after treatment

Your lips will usually feel firm, a little tender and warmer than usual, and they'll look fuller than the final result. Small pinpoint marks where the needle went in are normal, and there may be a little redness. This is the stage where lips can look quite dramatic, it settles.

First 24 to 48 hours: peak swelling

This is typically when swelling is at its most obvious. Lips can feel tight and look larger, and it's common for them to appear slightly uneven, because the two sides rarely swell at exactly the same rate. You might notice a little bruising too. This is the point people worry most, but it's usually the normal high-water mark rather than a problem.

Days 3 to 5: settling

The swelling starts to come down and the tightness eases. Your lips will still be a touch fuller than the end result, and any minor bruising begins to fade. Small lumps or firm areas that you can feel but not always see are common at this stage and usually soften on their own as everything settles.

Week 1 to 2: the final result

By the end of the first week most of the swelling has gone and your lips look much more like themselves. It's best to judge the final result at around two weeks, once the filler has fully settled and any residual puffiness has resolved. If you'd like to see how the lips look at that point, that's the right time for a review.

Normal versus signs to get checked

The vast majority of swelling is entirely normal. It helps to know what fits that picture and what should prompt a call.

Usually normal: tenderness, firmness, mild bruising, lips feeling tight or dry, and some day-to-day unevenness that improves as the swelling comes down.

Get in touch with your practitioner promptly if you notice:

  • Swelling that keeps increasing after the first two to three days rather than easing.
  • Severe pain, or pain that is getting worse instead of better.
  • Any blanching (white patches), or skin that looks dusky, grey or mottled in or around the lips.
  • A clear asymmetry that is still there at around two weeks.

Blanching, a dusky colour or severe worsening pain are uncommon, but they need to be assessed urgently by someone qualified to manage them. Because Eleana is an Advanced Nurse Practitioner and independent prescriber, the person who treats you is also the person able to review and manage any concern. If in doubt, always make contact rather than waiting.

Practical ways to reduce swelling

You can't switch swelling off, but a few simple things keep it down and more comfortable:

  • Cool compress. Hold a clean, cool compress gently against the lips in the first day or two. Cool, not frozen, and never pressed hard.
  • Sleep slightly elevated. An extra pillow for the first couple of nights helps fluid drain away rather than pooling in your lips overnight.
  • Stay hydrated. Drinking plenty of water supports normal healing.
  • Avoid alcohol, heat and hard exercise early on. Alcohol, hot baths, saunas and strenuous workouts all increase blood flow and can make swelling and bruising worse in the first day or two.

Your practitioner will give you full aftercare guidance for your treatment, and there's more detail in our lip filler aftercare guide. You can also read about the treatment itself on our lip fillers page.

Be patient with the result

The single most useful thing to remember is not to judge your lips in the first few days. Swelling makes them look bigger and sometimes uneven, and that changes a lot as it settles. Give it a full two weeks before deciding how you feel about the shape and volume. If you have questions along the way, you're always welcome to get in touch.

Common questions

How long does lip filler swelling last?

Most swelling peaks in the first 24 to 48 hours and then eases over the following days. For most people the lips look much more settled by the end of the first week, with the final result usually clear at about two weeks.

Is it normal for lips to look uneven after filler?

Some unevenness in the first few days is common, because swelling and any small bruising rarely settle at exactly the same rate on both sides. This usually balances out as the swelling goes down. If a clear asymmetry is still there at around two weeks, contact your practitioner for a review.

How can I reduce swelling after lip filler?

A cool compress held gently against the lips, sleeping with your head slightly elevated, staying hydrated and avoiding alcohol, heat and hard exercise for the first day or two all help keep swelling down. Follow the aftercare advice your practitioner gives you.

When should I be worried about swelling?

Contact your practitioner urgently if you have severe or worsening pain, swelling that keeps increasing after the first couple of days, or any blanching, white patches or a dusky, mottled colour in or around the lips. These are uncommon but need prompt assessment by someone qualified to manage them.
