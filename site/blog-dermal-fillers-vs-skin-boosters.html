Dermal fillers vs skin boosters

Written by Eleana, Advanced Nurse Practitioner & independent prescriber

They sound similar and both involve injections, but dermal fillers and skin boosters do genuinely different jobs. One changes the shape of a specific area; the other improves the quality of your skin. Understanding the difference makes it much easier to choose what's actually right for you.

The core difference in one line

Dermal fillers add structure and volume to a specific place. Skin boosters improve skin quality across an area. That single distinction explains almost everything else — what they treat, how they look and how long they last.

What dermal fillers do

Dermal fillers are a firmer hyaluronic acid gel that sits in a targeted spot to add support, volume or definition. Hyaluronic acid is a sugar molecule that occurs naturally in the skin and holds water, which is part of why these gels integrate well and can be adjusted over time.

Because the gel holds its shape, fillers are used where you want a visible change to contour or projection. Common areas include:

  • Cheeks — restoring or refining volume and support
  • Chin and jawline — adding definition and balance to the profile
  • Lips — shape, border definition and subtle volume

The change is generally noticeable straight away, once any swelling settles. You can read more about how we approach this on our dermal fillers page.

What skin boosters do

Skin boosters work differently. Rather than sitting in one spot to build shape, they use a more fluid hyaluronic acid that is placed into the skin so it spreads through the tissue. The aim isn't to change your contours — it's to improve how the skin itself feels and behaves: hydration, smoothness, texture and a healthier-looking glow.

Some skin boosters are hyaluronic-acid based, while others in this family are polynucleotides, which work to support skin quality and condition over a short course of treatments. Neither is a prescription-only medicine. Skin boosters tend to suit people whose main concern is skin that feels dry, dull, rough or crepey rather than a lack of volume in one area. Typical treatment areas are the face, under-eye area, neck, décolletage and the backs of the hands.

Because they work by improving the skin gradually, results build over the course of treatment rather than appearing all at once. There's more detail on our skin boosters page.

How the results look and last

The two treatments produce quite different kinds of result:

Dermal fillers

A defined, structural change to a specific area — more volume in the cheeks, a sharper jaw, a better-balanced lip. Results are usually visible almost immediately and, depending on the product and area, commonly last from several months to well over a year.

Skin boosters

A subtle overall improvement in skin quality — skin that looks fresher, feels more hydrated and has a smoother texture. There's no change to your shape. Results appear gradually and are usually maintained with a short initial course followed by periodic top-ups.

Can you combine them?

Yes — and it's common, because they solve different problems. Someone might use dermal filler to restore support in the cheeks while using a skin booster to improve the quality of the skin over the wider face. The two complement each other rather than compete.

When treatments are combined, they're usually spaced across separate appointments so each area can settle and be assessed properly. There's no fixed formula; the right combination and order depend entirely on your skin and your goals.

How a consultation decides

The honest answer to "which one do I need?" is that it depends on what's bothering you. A consultation is where that gets worked out. It's an assessment of your skin and facial structure, a conversation about what you'd like to change, and a discussion of what is — and isn't — realistic.

As a nurse-led clinic, we'll only recommend a treatment if it genuinely suits your goal, and we'll say so if it doesn't. Sometimes the best plan is a filler, sometimes a skin booster, sometimes a combination over time, and occasionally neither. You can arrange a consultation through our contact page.

Common questions

Can I have dermal fillers and skin boosters at the same time?

They are often used together because they do different jobs, but they are usually done as separate appointments so each area can settle. Your plan and timings are agreed at a consultation.

Which one should I choose?

It depends on your goal. If you want to add volume or definition to a specific area, that points towards dermal fillers. If your skin feels dry, dull or crepey, a skin booster is usually the better fit. A consultation helps decide.

How long do the results last?

Dermal filler results commonly last several months to well over a year depending on the product and area. Skin boosters are typically given as a short course and topped up periodically, as results are more gradual.

Do they hurt?

Both involve fine injections and most people find them very manageable. Some tenderness, small bumps or bruising can occur and settle within a few days.
